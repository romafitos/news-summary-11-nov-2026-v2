World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. US-mediated Russia-Ukraine talks ended in Abu Dhabi agreeing a prisoner swap of 1,000 or 1,500 captives each, Zelenskyy said, but no ceasefire or territory deal; US mediators cited a lack of trust (Reuters). Kushner and Witkoff fly to Moscow on Thursday, then Dublin to meet Zelenskyy. Russia hit six power substations in central Ukraine, and a market strike in the Odesa region killed three.

At COP31 in Antalya, UN chief Antonio Guterres urged taxes on windfall profits, noting the nine biggest oil firms earned $95 billion in a quarter — about 14 hours of their profits would fund the $600 million Loss and Damage Fund (UN News). He proposed a "climate justice compact" and $1.3 trillion in climate finance.

The Justice Department charged three men over alleged Iran-backed plots, nearly 20 in Europe and the US, including RAF Fairford; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved to a military base after threats (New York Times). Iran negotiator Ghalibaf told Al Jazeera the Strait of Hormuz "will never return" to its pre-war state, as Tehran set seven conditions.

In Spain, four million workers joined the first general strike in 14 years over housing, ahead of a snap election on 29 November (Reuters). Average rent of 1,176 euros a month is 98.7 percent of a young worker's salary, the Youth Council says; Spain lacks 750,000 homes.

British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer posted to a NATO corps near Gloucester on suspicion of spying for Russia; the Kremlin denied any link on Wednesday (Reuters).

Russia's ambassador to the US, Alexander Darchiev, died suddenly in Moscow aged 66, the ministry said (Reuters). A spokeswoman said he died of a chronic illness; envoy since March 2025.

Kenya discharged its last Ebola patient and began a 42-day countdown to an Ebola-free declaration, expected 23 December, Health Secretary Aden Duale said (Reuters). The Bundibugyo outbreak: nine cases, seven recoveries, two deaths.
